Ryter opens on the workspace. Conversation, Hats, and Sessions are tabs on the top bar, the hat rack is the sidebar, and the instruments sit on the right. The ledger and the classic screen stay available.

## Upgrade

```sh
ryter update
```

Coming from 0.26.0:

- **The workspace is the screen you get.** `RYTER` sits on the top bar with Conversation, Hats, and Sessions (`Alt+1`, `Alt+2`, `Alt+3`, or a click when the mouse is on). The hat rack is the sidebar: the project, the branch, and the hats. The view you picked fills the middle. Instruments — activity, context, spend, files, and the plan — sit on the right. `F2` or `^b` hides that column. `F6` and `F7` move focus between the sidebar, the view, and the instruments.
- **Hats read as names, and the keys are the ones on the hint line.** `Tab` moves inside the mode you are in (plan and build, or audit and scribe). `Shift+Tab` switches mode. Hat names on this screen are capitalized (Plan, Build, Audit, Scribe); commands and config stay lowercase. `/` is the only command-palette key, including in `/help`. `Ctrl+P` does not open it.
- **The type is the terminal's own.** `RYTER` and the headings are the normal font. Chips, the active tab, and the active hat are solid blocks. `[ui] nerd_font` (default off) swaps a few marks — branch, folder, file, hat, status — for Nerd Font icons. `RYTER_NERD_FONT=1` does the same when the key is absent. A terminal does not report its font, so nothing turns the icons on by itself.
- **Ledger and classic are still there.** `[ui] layout` is `workspace`, `ledger`, or `classic`, including from `/settings`.

## Why

The workspace mockup was the screen to compare against, and then the screen to open on. PR #78 made that layout the default.

## Good to know

- A switch while a hat works is still the next message's hat only, as in 0.26.0.
- The workbench (`^t`) keeps the ledger's frame while it is open.
- `[ui] panel = false` still starts with the instruments column hidden.
