On a wide screen the hats are back at the left: from 150 columns the screen is three panes, the rail with the hats, the conversation centred, the sidebar with the measurements. And a hat switch while a turn runs is for your next message only: the hat that started the turn finishes it on its own model, and the screen says which hat is working.

## Upgrade

```sh
ryter update
```

Coming from 0.25.1:

- **Three panes from 150 columns.** A rail at the left carries `RYTER`, the session's title, the place and the hats ledger; the sidebar at the right carries `now`, context, spend, changes and permissions; the conversation sits centred between them with its 112-column cap. Both columns are 30 wide until the conversation has its 112, then grow to 40 with the screen, so the spare width goes to names and paths before it is air, and the air there is sits inside a frame on both sides. Between 100 and 149 columns the screen is as 0.25.1 had it, one sidebar with the conversation at the left; below 100 the status line. `^b` hides and shows both columns.
- **A switch while a hat works chooses the hat for your next message, and nothing else.** `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `/plan`, `/build` and `/scribe` during a turn move the prompt's chip, its rule and the ledger's `▸` to the hat you chose, at once. The hat whose model started the turn finishes it: its row keeps the spinner, the `now` block keeps its model and its verb, the status row keeps its colour, and what it writes is headed by its own name. When the turn ends the switch lands, and a message queued meanwhile goes to the new hat.

## Why

The user's run of 0.25.1 on a wide terminal with a model per hat. With the conversation at the left and one sidebar, "there is a huge blank space between the right edge of the chat and the right panel"; the screen "is designed and laid out for left and right panel unless it's a narrow screen". A capped reading column needs a frame on both sides, and one bar leaves a hole on whichever side the column is not. The threshold is 150 columns because two columns of 30 and the 120-wide screen's conversation make 150.

And `Tab` during a plan turn appeared to switch the plan onto build's model. Driven on the real binary, the agent had kept the plan on its own model to the end, and took the switch only when the turn ended, as it always had. The screen was the fault: it flipped at once, marked build as the hat working, named build's model in `now`, and signed the plan's reply with it. The user's call: "hold the switch till after the turn is finished". Two hats working at once is a later decision, not taken. `DECISIONS.md` 2026-10-11; `docs/sidebar-design.md` R-LAYOUT-04 and R-KEY-05, §17.

## Good to know

- Nothing about what the hats do changed, and no key changed. A switch between turns is as it was.
- The status line on a narrow screen names the hat working while a turn runs, and the hat on between turns.
- `[ui] panel = false` starts with both columns hidden.

## Tried before release

The real binary against the simulated provider: a plan turn with `Tab` pressed while it ran at 220×50 (the plan finished on its model and was signed by it; the follow-up went to the build hat's model); the screen at 120×40, 150×45, 176×45 and 220×50 with an ask card, a running command and `^b`; and 220×50 in sixteen colours. The golden snapshots at 160×50 are three panes; a test pins the columns at 100, 120, 149, 150, 176, 180, 196 and 220 columns, and another the switch during a turn.

A test fixture that started an audit's turn in the build hat, which the agent never does, now starts it in audit as `/audit` does.
