The model picker lists only the OpenRouter models your account can use. The crew won't start if your privacy settings rule out one of its seats. And work a builder has finished is kept when a later model can't be reached.

## Upgrade

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zypher-systems/ryter/main/install.sh | sh
```

## What was wrong

A real crew run cost $4.67 and landed nothing:
- **The architect** (claude-opus-5.5) planned the work for $2.56.
- **The builder** (claude-sonnet-5.5) built it for $1.86.
- **The auditor** (claude-fable-5.1) was refused on its first call. OpenRouter said the account's zero data retention setting left the model no provider. The refusal cost nothing, but it came last.

Three things let that happen:
- **The picker listed models the account can't use.** It showed OpenRouter's whole catalog, so a model your privacy settings rule out could be picked for a seat.
- **Nothing checked the seats before the crew started.** The auditor is called last, so its refusal came after the other seats had been paid.
- **The refusal deleted the builder's work.** Any failed model call during a task removed the task's branch, including an auditor's refusal that was no verdict on the work. The task's reason was OpenRouter's raw JSON.

The prices were right. They matched OpenRouter's list to the cent.

## What changed

- **The picker lists what your account can use.** Ryter now asks OpenRouter for the models your account allows (`/models/user`), which applies your privacy settings, provider rules, and guardrails. On the account above, that hides 97 models, including every Fable model, OpenAI's o-series, and the `-pro` models. The same list feeds the crew builder, the ready-made crews, and `ryter models`. OpenRouter's `~…-latest` aliases now show too. If that list fails to load, Ryter falls back to the full list of models that take tools.
- **The crew checks its seats before it starts.** Before the architect or a builder runs, Ryter asks OpenRouter which of the seats it's about to use your account rules out. This lookup is free. If any are ruled out, nothing runs, the tasks stay queued, and you see which seat to change:

  ```
  The crew did not start: this account can't use
    auditor: anthropic/claude-fable-5.1
  … Give the seat another model in /crew, or change the settings at
  https://openrouter.ai/settings/privacy. The tasks stay queued.
  ```

  A design you asked to hold before building checks only the architect, so it isn't blocked by an auditor you'll fix later.
- **A paused crew stays paused for the rest of the turn.** Before, the lead ran the crew again after each of its replies, paying for each round up to the round limit. This also applied to builds that paused for want of an independent auditor.
- **Finished work is kept.** If the auditor can't be reached, the task stops and says why, and its branch keeps the builder's work. The same goes for any other model call that fails after the builder has committed. A retry picks up from that branch.
- **Refusals read as sentences,** with the privacy settings link, on the board, in the lead's report, and in the error line.

## Tried before release

In the TUI, on OpenRouter, with the lead on grok-4.7 and an account that has zero data retention on:
- **`ryter models openrouter`** listed 281 models in 0.32 s, with no Fable model. Before, it listed 295, Fable included.
- **In `/models`,** searching `fable` found nothing. Searching `claude` listed Opus and Sonnet 5.5 and the `~anthropic/…-latest` aliases, all priced.
- **In crew mode, with the auditor set to claude-fable-5.1,** asking for "a subtract function, designed by the architect first, then built" stopped the crew before the architect ran. The message above named the auditor. The spend log had six lead calls ($0.03) and no crew calls, and the design task stayed queued.
- **After switching the auditor back to qwen3.8-max-prime,** saying continue ran the queued design. The builder built it, the auditor passed it, and it landed on `master` as one commit with its tests passing. That cost $0.17.
